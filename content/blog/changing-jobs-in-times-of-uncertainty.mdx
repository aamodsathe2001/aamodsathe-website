---
title: "Changing jobs in times of uncertainty"
date: "2020-04-28"
description: "What I learned about navigating career transitions during one of the most uncertain periods in recent history."
---

So I just changed jobs. Yes; in the middle of COVID crisis, while sheltering in place, I thought to myself, what would make this experience even more interesting, oh yeah, let me leave my current job and get into a brand new challenging role!!!!

Ofcourse it didnt happen that way! As with many career moves this was in the works for a bit. The timing just happened to be in the middle of this crisis. And I wasnt going far or taking any role risk per say (same industry, similar profile, same geographic area etc.), but it was still nerve wracking. So I thought I will write down a few of my thoughts as I went through this process incase they help anyone else.

But before that, I have to say something about the place I am leaving. I have been incredibly lucky to have had an opportunity to work at Google. To be part of this incredible company, to be able to work with some of the smartest people on earth and to be forever counted as 'Googler' (although with an 'ex' now). I am humbled and forever thankful for this incredible experience. And I will miss working at Google and most importantly I will miss my team dearly!

But having said that, we all have to look at our careers as a journey. Its not a snapshot, its not a point in time and it certainly is not ever steady 'up and to the right'. So how should we be thinking about it? What are some of the things that are ever lasting and will always help us -- health/economic crisis or not. Here are a few things that helped me. Maybe they will help you too.

**1- Know where you are going:** Like I said, careers are not destinations, but journeys . It is very important to have a sense of where you are going. By definition, you are not there yet. But are you moving in the right direction? Are you learning that skill that will get you there? Are you making the connections that will get you there? Introspect and talk to a lot of people to arrive at answers to those questions.

**2- Know what you will do after you next step:** This is so important. As you are thinking about your careers, and especially when you are thinking of a change, have atleast a vague idea of what the next step means in the long term. Where will you go after that? Why are you really making the change? Sometimes when we are just bored of doing the same things, some other role may sound very exciting initially. But it is not going to take you far. Dont fall in that trap.

3- **Career changes are tough**: Always! No matter who you are or what the external situation is. Maybe early on in your careers its easy to switch roles, companies etc. But as you gain more experience in your fields, moves get tougher and tougher. Dont be hard on yourself. I used to be devastated looking at linkedin profiles and thinking, "I wanted THAT role". Talk to a lot of people at different companies. You will realize that its not just tough for you. Its tough for even the smartest, best people in a field. Its a natural process if you think about it. If you know you can easily get X, then your mind naturally wants to get X+1. And that can always be tough. Be patient. Think of #1 and #2 above when you are evaluating your next move. It helps put in perspective the inevitable failures and rejections along the way.

4- **Network, network and network**: I know it is probably the oldest cliche in business. But as you grow in your career you start to get much more value out of your network. This IS the primary way you will grow yourself. You might pick a thing or two from a book or a podcast, but you will really learn a lot from experiences of other people. So talk to a lot of people; in AND out of your close circles. The intangible benefits are countless, but more often than not, that one idea that you may pick from a colleague might just land you that next role too!

And most importantly, stay safe, take care of yourself and your loved ones. Never let a good crisis go to waste! And that too a crisis that forces you to spend more time with your family!! If you dont come out of this with a new skill or perspective then you will know that time was never your enemy (I will give a big break to parents from that last statement :)).
